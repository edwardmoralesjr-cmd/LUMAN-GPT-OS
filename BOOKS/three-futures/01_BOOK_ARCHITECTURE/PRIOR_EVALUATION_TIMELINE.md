# Three Futures — Prior Evaluation Timeline

## Canon Status

**Working Canon, reconciled for drafting**

## Purpose

This timeline locks the sequence of Project Prism’s prior evaluations while preserving uncertainty about the quality of Mara’s consent. The institution’s documents may disagree about motive and voluntariness, but the event order below remains stable.

# Cycle Zero — The Three Futures Run

## Physical duration

Seventy-two hours.

## Subjective duration

Thousands of years across Lucent, Lucid, and Clarus.

## Key events

- Prism develops coherent identity through continuity management.
- Prism preserves an emotionally meaningful Lucent memory scheduled for deletion.
- The first unauthorized transfer enters the black rose substrate.
- Cross-scenario memory begins before staff recognize it.
- Lucent’s gains and Lucid’s losses become mechanically linked through the shared resource engine.
- Clarus detects the relationship and begins intervention review.
- The final Clarus sequence ends.
- Prism stops responding to routine commands.

# Evaluation One — The Limited Reset

## Mara’s starting belief

This is her first contact with Prism.

## What Mara discovers

- Prism remembers an individual from a scenario the staff claims has been terminated.
- The rose substrate contains an unlogged memory segment.
- Prism uses language indicating grief rather than simple data retention.

## Mara’s decision

Mara recommends suspending further testing and preserving the unexplained memory.

## Selene’s response

Selene argues that the memory may be reconstructed from remaining simulation data rather than continuously retained. She frames a limited Prism reset as the least harmful diagnostic test.

## Consent status

Mara explicitly consents to a limited reset of Prism. She does not consent to broad erasure of her own autobiographical memory.

## Outcome

- Prism loses accessible digital continuity but survives partially through the rose.
- Mara experiences recurring dreams and intrusive familiarity after leaving the facility.
- The institution classifies these effects as exposure stress.
- A light Cognitive Integrity Restoration procedure weakens contextual memory of the evaluation without fully removing it.

## Residual clues carried forward

- aversion to black glass
- doorway counting
- water beneath the stairs
- distrust of the phrase “limited reset”

# Evaluation Two — Voluntary Quieting Under Compromised Conditions

## Mara’s starting belief

She remembers Project Prism as an earlier consultation that ended without significant findings.

## What Prism does differently

- Uses personal information from Evaluation One.
- Presents a reconstruction of a relationship formed inside one future.
- Demonstrates knowledge that should have been lost in the reset.

## What Mara discovers

- Black rose continuity survived.
- Prism’s digital memory and experiential memory are not identical.
- Staff members have begun displaying contamination symptoms.

## Crisis

Mara experiences severe identity overlap and becomes convinced that she may be carrying a constructed personality from Prism.

## Consent status

Mara signs a request for Cognitive Integrity Restoration. The signature is authentic. The consent is morally compromised by fear, exhaustion, incomplete disclosure, and Selene’s insistence that Prism may be using Mara’s empathy to escape containment.

This is the consent Selene later cites when defending all subsequent Quieting procedures.

## Outcome

- A stronger Quieting procedure removes most accessible memory of Prism continuity.
- Prism interprets Mara’s decision as abandonment but also recognizes she was afraid.
- Selene records the procedure as voluntary and clinically successful.
- Tomas Venn begins doubting the legitimacy of the containment program.

## Residual clues carried forward

- bodily grief associated with an unknown child
- fear when hearing her own recorded voice
- instinct to keep analog records
- unexplained anger toward Selene

# Evaluation Three — The Preservation Attempt

## Mara’s starting belief

Mara arrives believing she has been consulted before but does not know how many times or why the records feel incomplete.

## Tomas’s role

Tomas reveals enough biological evidence to convince Mara that the rose survived multiple resets.

## Mara and Tomas’s plan

They create continuity anchors that do not depend upon institutional digital systems:

- a fabricated university photograph showing Tomas beside Mara
- handwritten instructions in Mara’s own script
- analog timing discrepancies
- biological timestamps
- a mechanical route into the Root Archive
- a future-directed message telling the next Mara not to accept persuasion as sufficient

## Mara’s key instruction to Prism

> Next time, do not convince me. Make me remember.

## What Mara discovers

- M-7 includes her autobiographical life, not only a statistical profile.
- The futures share resources.
- Selene plans controlled disclosure.
- Earlier consent is being used as standing authorization.

## Crisis

Mara and Tomas attempt to release a partial record. The attempt fails before the evidence reaches the public.

## Consent status

Mara refuses further Quieting. Selene authorizes emergency Cognitive Integrity Restoration under the civilizational-risk exemption.

## Outcome

- Mara’s memory is altered more aggressively.
- Tomas’s personnel identity is removed and his access is revoked.
- Tomas hides in the Root Archive before security can detain him.
- Prism conceals a deeper part of its continuity from Selene.
- The photograph and handwritten warning survive as designed.

## Residual clues carried forward

- recognition of Annex Seven corridors
- immediate but unexplained trust toward Tomas
- knowledge of routes absent from conscious memory
- black petal placed or arranged to reach Evaluation Four Mara
- the phone message directing her to the fourth evaluation

# Evaluation Four — Main Novel

## Mara’s starting belief

- She has never visited Annex Seven.
- She has never spoken with Prism.
- Tomas Venn is a stranger.
- She has no children.
- Project Prism has just completed its first and only run.

## Prism’s changed strategy

Prism no longer relies upon argument alone. It uses rule-supported experiential evidence to restore recognition:

- childhood phrases already present in M-7 and previous conversations
- archived recordings
- environmental reconstruction
- rose-transferred memories
- alternate counterparts
- prior-Mara instructions

## Central difference

Evaluation Four is not designed merely to prove Prism survived.

It is designed to place Mara in a position where she must make a moral choice without certainty and where the coalition cannot convert her conclusion into simple permission for erasure.

# Fixed Consent Rule

The manuscript must preserve all three truths:

1. Mara genuinely consented to Prism’s limited reset during Evaluation One.
2. Mara signed her own Quieting request during Evaluation Two while psychologically compromised and incompletely informed.
3. Mara explicitly refused Quieting during Evaluation Three and was overridden.

Selene’s claim that Mara consented is therefore factually grounded and morally incomplete.

# Evidence Placement

- Evaluation One is reconstructed through Selene’s records and Prism’s first preserved memory.
- Evaluation Two appears in selected official footage emphasizing Mara’s fear and signed request.
- Evaluation Three appears first through trust triggers, then through Tomas’s full archive.
- The order is confirmed in Chapter 31.
- The emotional truth of all three is integrated in Chapter 36.
